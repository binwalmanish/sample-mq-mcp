How to Read This Document

This document follows a phased, reality-first approach to MQ SLI/SLO definition. The philosophy:

  • Start from what we can measure today, not what we wish we could
  • Set baselines from observed behaviour, not aspirational targets
  • Identify gaps honestly — gaps are a deliverable, not a failure
  • Infrastructure first, application later — clearly separated
Why now? We currently have no defined SLIs or SLOs for MQ infrastructure. This makes it impossible to objectively answer customer questions about reliability, communicate impact during incidents, or justify capacity investment. This document begins that definition in a structured, low-risk, phased way.

1

Infrastructure SLI Identification

Timeline: Days 1–3  |  Goal: Define what to measure

1.1 Core SLI Categories

Every SLI falls into one of five categories. This is the starting framework — not every category needs a fully instrumented SLI on day one.

Category Question It Answers Priority Banking Relevance
Availability Is MQ infrastructure up and serving? P1 — Start Here Regulatory requirement; payment rails cannot go down
Latency How fast do messages flow through MQ? P2 Real-time fraud detection, trade settlement depend on low latency
Reliability How often do failures, retries, or disruptions occur? P2 Crash frequency impacts audit trails and DORA metrics
Capacity Are queues backing up? Is processing saturated? P2 Queue saturation = direct MQRC_Q_FULL, blocking transactions
Connectivity Are channels and connections healthy? P3 Channel failures prevent inter-system message flow between BU systems

1.2 Candidate SLIs

The first draft. These are candidates — not all will survive baselining. The goal is to get a measurable list on paper, not to achieve perfection.

SLI-1 Queue Manager Availability Availability
DescriptionPercentage of time each Queue Manager is up and accepting connections
MeasurementPeriodic health probe every 60 s — QM responds to administrative command (PING QMGR) or synthetic connection
Good EventQM responds successfully within defined timeout
Bad EventQM unresponsive, process not running, or probe timeout
Unit% uptime per QM (rolling 30-day window)
FormulaGood Probes / Total Probes × 100
MQ Commandsrunmqsc <QMGR> <<< "PING QMGR"  |  dspmq -m <QMGR>
Why this matters: If the QM is down, nothing works. This is the single most important indicator. In banking, even a 60-second QM outage during market hours can cause missed trades or payment failures.
SLI-2 Channel Availability Connectivity
DescriptionPercentage of expected channels that are in active/running state
MeasurementChannel status polling every 60–120 s via DISPLAY CHSTATUS(*)
Good EventChannel in RUNNING state (or INACTIVE for on-demand channels)
Bad EventChannel in STOPPED, RETRYING, or BINDING for > 3 minutes
Unit% active channels vs expected count per QM
FormulaRunning Channels / Expected Channels × 100
MQ CommandsDISPLAY CHSTATUS(*) STATUS  |  DISPLAY CHLAUTH(*)
Why this matters: Channels are the communication backbone. A stopped channel means messages aren't flowing between systems. In a banking context this means inter-bank settlement or core-banking integration is silently broken.
Prerequisite: Requires an inventory of expected channels per QM. If this doesn't exist, it's a Gap (logged in Phase 2). Target: resolve via DISPLAY CHANNEL(*) export + configuration management tooling.
SLI-3 Message Throughput Latency Latency
DescriptionTime for a message to transit through MQ — from MQPUT to availability for MQGET
MeasurementSynthetic test messages on monitoring queues, or timestamp-based comparison where available (MQMD.PutDateTime vs. Get time)
Good EventMessage available within latency threshold
Bad EventMessage exceeds threshold
UnitMilliseconds (local QM) / Seconds (remote/cluster)
FormulaP99(MQGET_time − MQPUT_time) per monitoring queue, per 5-min window
MQ Commandsamqsput / amqsget with timestamp logging  |  DISPLAY QSTATUS(*) MSGAGE for queue age
Why this matters: Slow message delivery degrades downstream applications even when MQ is technically "up". Fraud detection engines and real-time payment systems have hard SLA requirements on message age.
Note: This is intra-MQ latency only. End-to-end application latency (including consumer processing time) is out of scope for Infrastructure SLIs and deferred to a future Application SLO phase.
SLI-4 Queue Depth Threshold Breach Capacity
DescriptionPercentage of time monitored queues remain below a defined depth threshold (e.g. 80% of MAXDEPTH)
MeasurementQueue depth polling every 30 s via DISPLAY QSTATUS(*) CURDEPTH
Good EventQueue depth < threshold at sample time
Bad EventQueue depth ≥ threshold at sample time
Unit% of time below threshold, per queue (aggregated per QM)
FormulaSamples below threshold / Total samples × 100    Threshold = CURDEPTH / MAXDEPTH < 0.80
MQ CommandsDISPLAY QSTATUS(APP.*) CURDEPTH MSGAGE  |  DISPLAY Q(*) MAXDEPTH
Why this matters: A queue nearing MAXDEPTH will start rejecting new messages (MQRC_Q_FULL = 2053), causing direct application impact. Measuring headroom catches problems before they become outages. In banking, a full queue on a payment flow is a P1 incident.
SLI-5 Connection Failure Rate Connectivity
DescriptionProportion of client connection attempts that fail due to MQ infrastructure issues
MeasurementConnection log events, channel status events, or application-reported metrics from SYSTEM.ADMIN.QMGR.EVENT queue
Good EventConnection established successfully (MQRC = 0, MQCC_OK)
Bad EventConnection rejected, timed out, or returned MQRC including 2035 (MQRC_NOT_AUTHORIZED), 2059 (MQRC_Q_MGR_NOT_AVAILABLE), 2085 (MQRC_UNKNOWN_OBJECT_NAME)
Unit% of connection attempts that result in infrastructure-caused failure (per QM, per 30-day window)
Formula(1 − Failed Infra Connections / Total Connections) × 100
MQ CommandsDISPLAY CONN(*) TYPE(CONN)  |  Scan AMQERR01.LOG for AMQ94xx events
Why this matters: Connection failures are the most common client-side complaint and often mask configuration issues or capacity problems. In banking, connection failures during market open can block thousands of transactions per minute.
Prerequisite: Requires per-QM event monitoring (MQ Event logging must be enabled: ALTER QMGR CONNEV(ENABLED)). Application-reported failures should be excluded if root cause is application credential issues.
SLI-6 Queue Manager Restart / Crash Count Reliability
DescriptionCount of unplanned Queue Manager restarts or crashes per QM per 30-day window
MeasurementQM process monitoring (process start/stop events), error log scanning (AMQERR01.LOG), OS-level process monitoring
Good EventQM running continuously with no unplanned restart
Bad EventUnplanned restart or crash detected (AMQ9999E in error log, or dspmq shows ENDED UNEXPECTEDLY)
UnitCount per QM per 30-day window (lower = better; target = 0)
FormulaCount of AMQ9999E / process restart events not tagged as planned maintenance
MQ Commandsgrep AMQ9999 /var/mqm/errors/AMQERR01.LOG  |  dspmq -xf -m <QMGR>
Why this matters: Frequent restarts indicate instability, missed maintenance, errant clients, or channel errors. Even a brief QM restart during banking hours causes message loss risk (non-persistent messages), in-flight transaction failures, and client reconnect storms.

Define Initial SLOs

Timeline: Days 6–8  |  Goal: Convert SLIs into SLOs based on current performance

3.1 The SLO Setting Principle

DO NOT set SLOs based on gut-feel or industry benchmarks alone.
DO use current baselines as the starting point, then define a realistic baseline + future target separately.

An SLO launched on Day 1 reflects current performance and provides a framework for improvement if unacceptable. An SLO target should be aspirationally better than the baseline — but never impossible to meet today. Once launched, the error budget drives improvement conversations naturally.

3.3 Example: How Baseline Drives SLO

Scenario: Setting SLO-1 (Queue Manager Availability)

You pull 4 weeks of daily availability data from your monitoring tool:

Week 1: QM-PROD-01=99.72% QM-PROD-02=99.43% QM-PROD-03=99.50% QM-PROD-04=99.83% Week 2: QM-PROD-01=99.91% QM-PROD-02=99.82% QM-PROD-03=99.77% QM-PROD-04=100.00% Week 3: QM-PROD-01=99.68% QM-PROD-02=99.91% QM-PROD-03=99.85% QM-PROD-04=99.94% Week 4: QM-PROD-01=99.55% QM-PROD-02=99.78% QM-PROD-03=99.62% QM-PROD-04=99.88% 30-day average per QM: PROD-01=99.72% PROD-02=99.74% PROD-03=99.69% PROD-04=99.91% Floor (worst QM): 99.69%

→ Set Initial SLO = 99.5% (conservative buffer below floor). This gives us room to absorb occasional incidents while still representing real performance.

→ Set Future Target SLO = 99.9% (reviewed at 90 days after Phase 5 dashboards are live).

→ Error Budget at 99.5% = 3.6 hours/30-days per QM. Budget burn rate alert at 5× (pages when burning budget in <7 days).

Structure the SLI/SLO Model

Timeline: Days 7–8  |  Goal: Define how SLOs are scoped, aggregated, and presented

4.1 SLO Scope Decision

Scope LevelRecommended?Notes
Per Queue Manager Yes — Start Here Most natural unit. Every QM has 1 set of SLOs. Aligns with MQ operational ownership. Simplest to baseline.
Per Team / Application Later Phases Requires application-to-QM ownership mapping. Deferred to Application SLO phase.
Per Region / Cluster Later Phases Requires multi-QM aggregation logic. Introduces weighting decisions. Phase 5+ work.
Per Environment Tier Phase 4 Ready Production vs. UAT vs. SIT vs. Dev. Different SLO targets per tier. Achievable in Phase 4.

4.2 Aggregation Logic (Future)

When aggregation is introduced (Phase 5+), define how individual QM performance rolls up to a single composite score:

MethodDescriptionProsCons
Average Composite = average availability across all QMs Simple, smooth data, easy to explain Hides individual failures; one bad QM is diluted
Min Composite = worst-performing QM only Conservative, protects against outliers, blunt but fair One bad QM drags the entire estate; may be overly pessimistic
Weighted Composite = weighted by traffic volume or business criticality Most accurate; reflects real business impact Requires ongoing criticality classification; maintenance overhead
Recommendation: Use weighted average once QMs are classified by business criticality tier (Tier 1 = Payment Processing, Tier 2 = Core Banking, Tier 3 = Internal Systems). Until classification is complete, report per-QM and avoid rolling up.

Development

5

Document, Publish & Review

Timeline: Day 10  |  Goal: Publish formal SLO document; create dashboards; schedule review cadence

5.1 Purpose

MQ infrastructure has operated without formal reliability guardrails — leaving teams unable to objectively answer:

"What level of reliability and availability does our IBM MQ infrastructure actually provide?"

This document defines — for the first time — Service Level Indicators (SLIs) and Service Level Objectives (SLOs) for IBM MQ infrastructure. It creates a shared language between MQ platform engineers, application teams, and banking stakeholders for discussing reliability.

5.2 Scope

In Scope (This Document)Out of Scope (Future Phase)
  • Queue Manager availability & health
  • Channel availability and connectivity
  • Queue depth and saturation metrics
  • Connection failure rates (infrastructure-caused)
  • QM crash / restart reliability
  • MQ log and process health
  • End-to-end business transaction SLIs (e.g. payment settlement time)
  • Application message processing SLIs (consumer processing time)
  • Network infrastructure SLIs (latency to/from MQ host)
  • Cost-per-transaction SLIs
  • Cross-platform integration SLIs (MQ to Kafka, MQ to TIBCO)
  • IBM MQ Appliance-specific SLIs
